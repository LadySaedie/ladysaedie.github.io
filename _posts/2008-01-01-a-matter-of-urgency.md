“You **do** know who’s up next, don’t you?”

“If memory serves, m’lud, the first case this afternoon is the divorce proceedings of one of m’lud’s favourite lady acquaintances, Mrs Anne Marlow.”

“Precisely, Beveridge! Anne. Marlow. Old flame. Soon to be unattached. Highly impressionable, and she isn't going to be highly impressed by me calling for order in my court by banging my fist on the bench, now is she? So don't just stand there gawping. Help me find my gavel!” 

“Begging m’lud’s pardon, but m’lud seemed quite pressed for time at the end of the session this morning and summarily dismissed the last case even before counsel had — how shall I say? — made the motion.”

“Yes, yes, yes, yes. So?” 

“Perhaps m’lud might like to recall this morning's events…”

“For heaven’s sake, Beveridge! Stop muttering and help me look! A good gavel is a good judge!” 

“Yes, m’lud. Was it not indeed a generous and munificent gesture of Ms Marlow to bake those dainties for m’lud’s morning tea?”

“Beveridge, what’s Ms Marlow’s muffins got to do with my gavel? Do you not understand the urgency of the situation? Mrs Marlow's case is up next, and the court is not possessed of its gavel! Now stop rambling about this morning’s muffins, and help me look!” 

“Indeed, m’lud. As I was saying, m’lud. Was it not entirely possible that out of deference to such an attentive courtesy paid by m’lud’s favourite lady acquaintance, that m’lud took it upon himself to consume all six bran muffins in one sitting?”

“Beveridge! For the last time, will you… Where are you going?”

“I believe it will be far quicker, m’lud, if I retrieve the gavel from m’lud’s WC myself.”
